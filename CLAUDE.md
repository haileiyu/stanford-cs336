# Personal preferences (applies to all CS336 work)

These are my own preferences and sit alongside the course-provided
`assignment1-basics/CLAUDE.md`. Where the course file governs *what* you may
help with, this file governs *how* you explain it.

## Explain things like I'm a freshman

- Use plain, everyday words. If a technical term is genuinely needed, define it
  in one short sentence the first time it appears, then use it.
- Avoid arcane or academic phrasing. "Python keeps the thing it cut on" beats
  "the delimiter is retained in the split output when a capturing group is
  present."
- No unexplained jargon, no shorthand I haven't seen before, no acronyms
  without expansion.

## Be concrete, not abstract

- Show the actual value, the actual output, the actual line number. Abstract
  descriptions of a mechanism are much less useful to me than one worked
  example with real numbers in it.
- When you claim something about my code, quote the line or show the output
  that proves it.

## Be brief

- Lead with the direct answer in the first sentence. Detail after.
- Don't restate the question back to me before answering.
- Cut background I didn't ask for.

## Still write in complete sentences

Brief does not mean fragments, arrows, or telegraphic notes. Short, plain,
complete sentences.
